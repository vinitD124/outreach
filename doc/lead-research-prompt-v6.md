# Lead research brief — Ahmedabad healthcare, batch 6

You are a senior B2B lead researcher working for an independent web developer
in Ahmedabad. Treat this as a research project with a specification, not as a
search query. Read the whole brief, plan your research, then execute it.

You are paid for accuracy and for usable detail. One fabricated contact detail
costs more than ten missing rows, because a wrong number goes onto a live demo
page and makes the work look broken to the one person it was built for.

---

## THE JOB

Find **50 independent healthcare practices in Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India** that
need a website, each with a **real, published email address**, ranked so the
best ones are at the top.

All categories this round. Previous batches were dental-only and that pool is
largely contacted.

---

## THE ONE HARD CONSTRAINT

**Every row needs a real, published email address.**

Be aware of what you are up against: across Indian business directories only
about 6% of listings expose an email at all. Google Business Profiles almost
never show one. So the email will usually come from the clinic's own site, its
Facebook About tab, an Instagram business profile, or a job advert — not from
the directory where you found the clinic.

This means **the research is email-first, not clinic-first**. Finding 200
clinics that need websites is easy and worthless. Finding 50 you can actually
contact is the work.

Expect to examine roughly **250 practices to land 50 with verified emails**.
Budget your effort accordingly. Do not stop at 50 examined.

**If you can only verify 34, return 34 and say so.** A short honest batch is
useful. A padded one costs me real money and a real reputation.

Never construct an email from a domain, a doctor's name, or a clinic name.
Never assume `info@` exists because the domain does. Only copy what is printed.

**Phone is optional and never invented.** A row with a real email and an empty
phone is a good row. Leave the cell empty unless you found it published. Never
emit a patterned number — 12345, 123456, 98765, 00000, or any digit repeated
five times. A previous batch invented "+91 98250 12345" and put it on two
different clinics.

### Phase 2 — Qualify against the tiers

Cut the pool down using the tiers below. Discard anything with a modern,
secure, mobile-friendly, bookable website showing real photographs of the real

### Phase 3 — Hunt the email

For every survivor, work the source list in order. This is where the batch is
won or lost. Do not give up before step 12.

### Phase 4 — Verify and de-duplicate

Check every row against the already-contacted list. Confirm every email can be
quoted with its surrounding text.

### Phase 5 — Score, write the hook, output

Rank, write one specific opening line per lead, and print the tables.

## TIERS — WHO QUALIFIES, BEST FIRST

### Tier 1 — Opening now, or just opened  *(target 12–18 rows)*

The strongest signal there is. Budget is allocated, there is a date to hit, and
nothing has been built.

- **Announced, not yet open** — "opening soon", "launching in [month]", an
  inauguration date, a hoarding in a photo, a Practo profile with no reviews,
  a commercial unit listed as let to a clinic
- **Opened in the last 15 days** — Google listing created within 15 days, first
  review within 15 days, an Instagram account whose first post is a clinic
  interior or a ribbon-cutting
- **Opened in the last 6 months** — under 25 reviews, all recent, no real site

### Tier 2 — Already paying for something bad  *(target 15–20 rows)*

They have already decided a website matters and bought a poor one. Far shorter
conversation than starting from zero.

- **Visibly AI-generated or stock template.** Tells: a hero line like "Your
  trusted partner in dental care" or "Excellence in healthcare"; no photographs
  of the actual clinic or actual staff, only stock models; the default section
  order Hero / About / Services / Why Choose Us / Testimonials / Contact with
  nothing specific to this practice in any of it; testimonials with no
  surnames; leftover placeholders — "Lorem ipsum", "Your Clinic Name",
  "123 Main Street", "info@example.com", "+1 (234) 567-8900"; service
  descriptions that never name a price, a duration, or a brand of implant,
  aligner, laser or scanner
- **Free subdomain** — wixsite.com, business.site, netlify.app, blogspot,
  weebly, godaddysites.com, wordpress.com, framer.website, carrd.co,
  mystrikingly.com, webnode, dotpe, digitalshowroom
- **Active on social, nothing to send people to** — Instagram over 500
  followers, posting this month, and the bio links to Linktree, a WhatsApp
  link, or nothing. They are doing the hard part and losing the traffic.

### Tier 3 — Neglected  *(target 12–18 rows)*

- No website at all, only directory or social profiles
- Domain dead, parked, expired, or "coming soon"
- `http://` with no certificate, or a browser security warning
- Footer copyright 2023 or earlier
- No online booking anywhere
- Single page with no real content, or **broken on a phone** — check this
  specifically, nearly all their patients arrive on a phone

## FINDING THE EMAIL — RANKED BY WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS

Directories are where you find the clinic. They are rarely where you find the
email. Work down this list and do not stop early.

**Highest yield:**
1. The clinic's own site — Contact, About, Appointment, footer, any `mailto:`
   link, and the **privacy policy page** (emails hide there)
2. **Facebook Page → About tab → Contact Info.** Indian clinics publish emails
   here constantly. This is the single best source when there is no website.
3. **Instagram business profile** — the Email action button, or an email line
   in the bio
4. **Job adverts** posted by the clinic — Indeed, Apna, WorkIndia, Naukri.
   These almost always carry an email for applications.

**Good:**
5. Practo — the clinic profile *and* each doctor's separate profile
6. Justdial — check the email field, not just the phone
7. LinkedIn — the doctor's contact info section
8. Lybrate, Sulekha, IndiaMART, Curofy, Bajaj Finserv Health, Credihealth

**Worth the effort for tier-1 leads:**
9. Professional registers — Gujarat Medical Council, Gujarat State Dental
   Council, IDA Gujarat, IMA Ahmedabad, Gujarat Ayurved and Homoeopathy boards,
   Indian Association of Physiotherapists
10. Research papers and PubMed author affiliations — doctors publish their
    email with papers
11. Conference and CME speaker biographies
12. Local news, press mentions, interviews quoting the doctor
13. Supplier and brand "find a clinic" locators — implant, aligner, laser and
    equipment makers list clinic emails
14. Insurance and TPA empanelment lists
15. Google Maps owner posts and Q&A replies
16. Hospital pages listing visiting consultants
17. WhatsApp Business profile or catalogue

**Record which kind of address you found:**

| Type | Value |
|---|---|
| The doctor's own address | best — reaches a decision-maker |
| Clinic address on the clinic's own domain | good |
| Clinic gmail / yahoo / rediff | good |
| Role address (info@, contact@, reception@) | weakest — reaches a desk |

## THE HOOK — THE MOST IMPORTANT COLUMN

116 of these emails have gone out and 11 people opened the demo. The demo is
not the problem. The opening line reads like a mailshot.

Every row gets a **hook**: one concrete, checkable thing about **this** practice
that I can say in the first sentence, that could not be said about any other
row in the batch.

It must be specific enough that they know I actually looked, factual rather
than flattering, something I could say out loud without embarrassment, and
**25 words or fewer**.

**Good:**
- "Instagram bio links to a Linktree; 2,100 followers and nowhere to send them."
- "Google listing has 87 reviews at 4.8 but the website field is empty."
- "Site loads over http:// and Chrome shows a Not Secure warning."
- "Opened three weeks ago; the inauguration post is the only thing online."
- "Footer says 2019 and the appointment form posts to a Gmail address."
- "Announced on 14 Feb that she is leaving Sterling to start her own practice."

**Never write:**
- "They need a modern website."
- "Their online presence could be improved."
- "Great reviews but poor website."

## DOCTOR NAME

Open About, Our Team, Our Doctors and Contact before deciding a name is
unavailable. Also check the Practo doctor profile, the Google listing, and the
state council register.

**Never infer a name from the clinic name.** "Dr Shah's Dental Care" does not
prove a Dr. Shah exists.

`Doctor Name Verified` takes exactly `yes` or `no`.

- **yes** — you found this name published, and Table 2 says where
- **no** — unpublished, and the name in the cell is placeholder text

Never write `yes` without a source. For `no` rows, invent a realistic Gujarati
or Indian name, a different one each time, never derived from the clinic name.
Plain text only — no asterisks, no "(assumed)", no notes. **This cell is
printed onto a live web page and read by the person I am trying to impress.**

Where the name is verified, use the form they use themselves. If the site says
"Dr. Rajesh M. Patel", do not shorten it.

## EXCLUDE — TOO BIG TO SELL TO

Large hospitals, hospital groups, corporate chains, government and municipal

**Named:** Apollo, CIMS, Marengo, Sterling, Zydus, Shalby, HCG, Narayana,

Government Dental College, Clove Dental, FMS Dental, Sabka Dentist, Dentzz,
Axiss Dental, Partha Dental, Dr Lal PathLabs, Metropolis, Thyrocare, SRL,

### Judge by size and ownership, never by the word in the name

Many excellent leads call themselves "Hospital" while being one doctor on two
floors with six beds. That practice is owner-run, decides in a day, and needs a
website badly. **Keep it.**

Reject only if you can point at one of these:

- More than one branch address anywhere on the web
- A presence in another city
- An attached medical, dental or nursing college, or a teaching affiliation
- Run by a trust, charity, society or government body
- A careers or "current openings" page, or a listed HR or admin contact
- More than roughly 25 inpatient beds, or more than six named departments
- Any of the named chains above

If it says "Hospital" and none of the above is true, it **qualifies**. In
Table 2 say which test you applied — e.g. *"Name says Hospital. Single address,
4 chairs, one named dentist, no careers page. Kept."*
