HOW TO FIND AN EMAIL WHEN THERE IS NO WEBSITE
================================================================================

This is the hard part and the reason most research fails. A clinic with no
website still usually publishes an email somewhere. Work this list IN ORDER
for every clinic before giving up. Do not stop at step 4.

   1. The clinic's own site: Contact, About, Appointment, footer, any
      mailto: link, and the privacy policy page (emails hide there)
   2. Google Business Profile — the listing often shows an email
   3. Practo clinic profile AND each doctor's separate Practo profile
   4. Justdial — check the email field, not just the phone
   5. Facebook Page, About tab, Contact Info. Indian clinics publish emails
      here constantly
   6. Instagram bio, or the Email action button on a business account
   7. LinkedIn — the doctor's contact info section
   8. Lybrate, Sulekha, IndiaMART, Curofy, Bajaj Finserv Health, Credihealth
   9. Professional registers: Gujarat Medical Council, Gujarat State Dental
      Council, Indian Dental Association Gujarat, Indian Medical Association
      Ahmedabad, Gujarat Ayurved / Homoeopathy boards, Indian Association of
      Physiotherapists
  10. Research papers and PubMed author affiliations — doctors publish their
      email with papers
  11. Conference and CME speaker biographies
  12. Local news, press mentions, and interview articles quoting the doctor
  13. Medical supplier and brand locators — implant, aligner, laser, and
      equipment makers list clinic emails in "find a clinic" pages
  14. Insurance and TPA empanelment lists
  15. Google Maps owner posts and Q&A replies
  16. Hospital or diagnostic-centre pages listing visiting consultants
  17. Job adverts posted by the clinic — Indeed, Apna, WorkIndia. These
      almost always carry an email for applications
  18. WhatsApp Business catalogue or profile

Only after all eighteen come up empty do you drop the clinic and find another.

EMAIL PREFERENCE ORDER — record which type you found:
  1. The doctor's own address (drname@gmail.com)          best
  2. A clinic address on the clinic's own domain
  3. A clinic gmail/yahoo/rediff address
  4. A generic role address (info@, contact@, reception@)  weakest
A personal address reaches a decision-maker. A role address reaches a desk.

THE HOOK — THE MOST IMPORTANT COLUMN ON THIS SHEET
================================================================================

116 of these emails have gone out and 11 people opened the demo. The problem
is not the demo. It is that the opening line reads like a mailshot.

So every row gets a HOOK: one concrete, specific, checkable thing about THIS
practice that I can say in the first sentence, that could not be said about
any other clinic in the batch.

It must be:
  - specific enough that they know I actually looked
  - factual, not flattery
  - something I could say out loud without embarrassment
  - 25 words or fewer

Good hooks:
  "Instagram bio links to a Linktree; 2,100 followers and nowhere to send them."
  "Google listing has 87 reviews at 4.8 but the website field is empty."
  "Site loads over http:// and Chrome shows a Not Secure warning."
  "Opened 3 weeks ago; the inauguration post is the only thing online."
  "Footer says 2019 and the appointment form goes to a Gmail address."
  "Announced on 14 Feb that she is leaving Sterling to start her own practice."

Bad hooks — never write these:
  "They need a modern website."
  "Their online presence could be improved."
  "Great reviews but poor website."
