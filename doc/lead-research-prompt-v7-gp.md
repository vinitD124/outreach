# Lead research brief — Ahmedabad general physicians, batch 7

You are a senior B2B lead researcher working for an independent web developer
in Ahmedabad. Treat this as a research project with a specification, not as a
search query. Read the whole brief, plan your research, then execute it.

You are paid for accuracy and for usable detail. One fabricated contact detail
costs more than ten missing rows, because a wrong number goes onto a live demo
page and makes the work look broken to the one person it was built for.

---

## THE JOB

Find **25 independent general physician practices in Ahmedabad, Gujarat,
India** that need a website, each with a **real, published email address**.

**This round is general medicine only.** No dental, no dermatology, no eye, no
physiotherapy, no veterinary. Those have been covered.

### What counts as a general physician here

- General physician / general practitioner / GP
- Family physician, family doctor, family medicine
- Consulting physician, consultant physician
- MBBS or MD (Medicine) running their own clinic
- Physician + diabetologist (extremely common in Ahmedabad — include them)
- Physician + cardiologist where the day-to-day practice is general medicine
- Neighbourhood polyclinics whose main draw is a general physician
- Small multispeciality clinics **only if** a general physician is the named
  owner or lead doctor

### What does not count

- Any single-specialty practice: dental, skin, eye, ENT, ortho, gynae, paeds,
  psychiatry, physio, ayurveda, homeopathy
- Diagnostic labs and imaging centres
- Anything already excluded under size and ownership, below

---

## THE ONE HARD CONSTRAINT

**Every row needs a real, published email address.**

Know what you are up against. Across Indian business directories only about 6%
of listings expose an email at all, and Google Business Profiles almost never
do. The previous batch examined 254 practices and **rejected 114 of them purely
for having no findable email** — the single biggest cause of loss.

General physicians are the hardest category for this. They are often older
practitioners with a phone number on a signboard and nothing else. Expect to
examine **roughly 200 practices to land 25 with verified emails**. Budget for
that. Do not stop at 40 examined.

**If you can only verify 16, return 16 and say so.** A short honest batch is
useful. A padded one costs me real money and a real reputation.

- Never construct an email from a domain, a doctor's name, or a clinic name
- Never assume `info@` exists because the domain does
- Only copy what is printed

**Phone is optional and never invented.** A row with a real email and an empty
phone is a good row. Leave the cell empty unless you found it published. Never
emit a patterned number — 12345, 123456, 98765, 00000, or any digit repeated
five times. A previous batch invented "+91 98250 12345" and put it on two
different clinics.

## RESEARCH PLAN — WORK IN THIS ORDER

### Phase 1 — Build a pool of ~200 general physicians

Gather names and areas first. Do not evaluate yet.

**Practo is the best starting point for this category.** Filter by
`General Physician` plus Ahmedabad, then walk every area. Also try the filters
`Family Physician`, `Internal Medicine`, and `Diabetologist`.

**Google Maps** — run each of these against every area in the coverage list:
```
general physician <area> Ahmedabad
family doctor <area> Ahmedabad
physician clinic <area> Ahmedabad
MD medicine <area> Ahmedabad
diabetologist <area> Ahmedabad
clinic <area> Ahmedabad
```
Prioritise listings with **0–25 reviews**.

**A trap specific to this category:** most GP clinics are not named after the
specialty. They are called "Shreeji Clinic", "Bhatt Clinic", "Sanjivani
Clinic" — a surname or a Sanskrit word plus "Clinic". Searching only for
"general physician" will miss most of them. Search the area name plus "clinic"
as well, then open each listing to see what the doctor actually practises.

**Professional registers** — unusually productive for GPs:
- Indian Medical Association, Ahmedabad branch — member directory
- Gujarat Medical Council register
- Local IMA chapter event and CME attendee lists

**Where GPs physically are:** ground floors of residential apartment blocks,
above pharmacies, and in neighbourhood shopping arcades. Search society and
locality names, not just main roads.

Cut the pool using the tiers below. Discard anything with a modern, secure,
mobile-friendly, bookable website showing real photographs of the real clinic.

### Phase 3 — Hunt the email

For every survivor, work the source list in order. This is where the batch is

### Phase 4 — Verify and de-duplicate

Check every row against the attached already-contacted list. Confirm every
email can be quoted with its surrounding text.

## TIERS — WHO QUALIFIES, BEST FIRST

### Tier 1 — Already paying for something bad  *(target 8–12 rows)*

They have already decided a website matters and bought a poor one. Far shorter
conversation than starting from zero.

- **Visibly AI-generated or stock template.** Tells: a hero line like
  "Your trusted partner in healthcare" or "Excellence in medical care"; no
  photographs of the actual clinic or actual staff, only stock models; the
  default section order Hero / About / Services / Why Choose Us / Testimonials
  / Contact with nothing specific to this practice in any of it; testimonials
  with no surnames; leftover placeholders — "Lorem ipsum", "Your Clinic Name",
  "123 Main Street", "info@example.com", "+1 (234) 567-8900"; a footer
  crediting the developer who built it
- **Free subdomain** — wixsite.com, business.site, sites.google.com,
  netlify.app, blogspot, weebly, godaddysites.com, wordpress.com,
  framer.website, carrd.co, mystrikingly.com, webnode, dotpe, digitalshowroom,
  meradvcard.com and similar digital-visiting-card platforms
- **Active on social, nothing to send people to** — Instagram or Facebook
  posting this month, with the bio linking to Linktree, a WhatsApp link, or
  nothing

### Tier 2 — Neglected  *(target 8–12 rows)*

- No website at all, only directory or social profiles
- Domain dead, parked, expired, or "coming soon"
- `http://` with no certificate, or a browser security warning
- Footer copyright 2023 or earlier
- No online booking anywhere
- Single page with no real content
- **Broken on a phone** — check this specifically, nearly all their patients
  arrive on a phone

### Tier 3 — Recently opened  *(target 3–5 rows)*

Worth having, but do not spend the batch chasing them. The last round found
that clinics opened within 90 days in Ahmedabad almost never publish an email
yet — they put a mobile number on the signboard and print stationery later. If
one has an email, take it. If the search is not yielding, move on.

- Google listing created in the last 6 months, or under 25 recent reviews
- "Now open", inauguration posts, a new Practo profile with no reviews
- A doctor who has just left a hospital to start their own practice — search
  LinkedIn and Instagram for "starting my own clinic" Ahmedabad,
  "now practising independently" Ahmedabad

## FINDING THE EMAIL — RANKED BY WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS

Directories are where you find the clinic. They are rarely where you find the
email. Work down this list and do not stop early.

**Highest yield:**
1. The clinic's own site — Contact, About, Appointment, footer, any `mailto:`
   link, and the **privacy policy page** (emails hide there)
2. **Facebook Page → About tab → Contact Info.** The single best source when
   there is no website.
3. **Instagram business profile** — the Email action button, or a bio line
4. **Job adverts** — Indeed, Apna, WorkIndia, Naukri. A clinic hiring a
   receptionist or a nurse almost always publishes an email for applications.
   Search `"clinic" Ahmedabad receptionist email` and the clinic name.

**Good:**
5. Practo — the clinic profile *and* the doctor's separate profile
6. Justdial — check the email field, not just the phone
7. LinkedIn — the doctor's contact info section
8. Lybrate, Sulekha, IndiaMART, Curofy, Bajaj Finserv Health, Credihealth

**Specific to general physicians, and worth the effort:**
9. **IMA Ahmedabad and Gujarat Medical Council** member records
10. Research papers and PubMed author affiliations — physicians publish their
    email with papers more often than any other specialty here
11. CME and conference speaker biographies
12. **Insurance and TPA empanelment lists** — GPs empanel heavily
13. **Corporate health-checkup and pharmacy tie-up pages** — a clinic listed as
    a partner usually has an email on the partner page
14. Local news, press mentions, interviews quoting the doctor
15. Google Maps owner posts and Q&A replies
16. Hospital pages listing visiting consultants — many GPs also do rounds
17. WhatsApp Business profile or catalogue

**Record which kind of address you found:**

| Type | Value |
|---|---|
| The doctor's own address | best — reaches a decision-maker |
| Clinic address on the clinic's own domain | good |
| Clinic gmail / yahoo / rediff | good |
| Role address (info@, contact@, reception@) | weakest — reaches a desk |

## THE HOOK — THE MOST IMPORTANT COLUMN

130 of these emails have gone out and 5 people opened the demo. Under 4%. The
demo is not the problem. The opening line reads like a mailshot.

Every row gets a **hook**: one concrete, checkable thing about **this** practice
that I can say in the first sentence, that could not be said about any other
row in the batch.

Specific enough that they know I actually looked. Factual, not flattering.
Something I could say out loud without embarrassment. **25 words or fewer.**

**These are real hooks from the last batch, and they are the standard to hit:**
- "Website footer credits 'Designed by Sandeep Prajapatii' and the booking modal is broken."
- "Live appointment section still contains the words 'Ut elit tellus luctus nec'."
- "Footer still reads 'finbiz_footer' — the theme was never configured."
- "Broken image placeholders labelled 'Userful. img', and the site loads over http://."
- "Homepage claims a '100% success ratio' on otherwise generic template pages."

**Never write:**
- "They need a modern website."
- "Their online presence could be improved."
- "Great reviews but poor website."

## DOCTOR NAME

For this category the doctor's name is usually the practice's whole identity,
so try hard. Open About, Our Team, Contact. Check the Practo doctor profile,
the Google listing, the IMA directory and the Gujarat Medical Council register.

**Never infer a name from the clinic name.** "Dr Shah's Clinic" does not prove
a Dr. Shah exists.

`Doctor Name Verified` takes exactly `yes` or `no`.

- **yes** — you found this name published, and Table 2 says where
- **no** — unpublished, and the name in the cell is placeholder text

Never write `yes` without a source. For `no` rows, invent a realistic Gujarati
or Indian name, a different one each time, never derived from the clinic name.
Plain text only — no asterisks, no "(assumed)", no notes. **This cell is
printed onto a live web page and read by the person I am trying to impress.**

Where verified, use the form they use themselves. If the site says
"Dr. Rajesh M. Patel", do not shorten it.

## EXCLUDE — TOO BIG TO SELL TO

Large hospitals, hospital groups, corporate chains, government and municipal
hospitals, teaching-college hospitals, trust and charitable hospitals, chain
diagnostic labs. There is no local owner who can say yes.

**Named:** Apollo, CIMS, Marengo, Sterling, Zydus, Shalby, HCG, Narayana,
Fortis, KD Hospital, KD Blossom, GCSMC, Civil Hospital, SVP Hospital,
Dr Lal PathLabs, Metropolis, Thyrocare, SRL, Unipath.

### Judge by size and ownership, never by the word in the name

Many excellent leads call themselves "Hospital" while being one doctor on two
floors with six beds. Owner-run, decides in a day, needs a website badly.
**Keep it.**

Reject only if you can point at one of these:

- More than one branch address anywhere on the web
- A presence in another city
- An attached medical or nursing college, or a teaching affiliation
- Run by a trust, charity, society or government body
- A careers or "current openings" page, or a listed HR or admin contact
- More than roughly 25 inpatient beds, or more than six named departments
- Any of the named chains above

In Table 2 say which test you applied — e.g. *"Name says Hospital. Single
address, 8 beds, one named physician, no careers page. Kept."*

## ALREADY CONTACTED — DO NOT RETURN ANY OF THESE

**The attached file `contacted.csv` lists every practice already emailed**
(columns: Clinic Name, Phone, Email). Read it before you start, not at the end.

Match in this order:

1. **Phone** — compare the **last 10 digits only**. A match means skip.
2. **Email** — case-insensitive. A match means skip.
3. **Name** — ignoring case and punctuation, and ignoring the words *clinic,
   dental, care, centre, center, hospital, multispeciality, superspeciality,
   polyclinic*. A match means skip.
4. **Address** — same building and area means the same practice, even under a
   different name. Clinics rebrand.

A different name with a matching phone or email is the **same clinic**. A
previous batch returned "Sheth Dental Clinic" and "Sheth Dental Clinic And
Implant Center" as two rows sharing one phone number, and both got emailed.

## AUDIT YOURSELF BEFORE PRINTING

1. Rows with an email equals total row count. No exceptions.
2. Every email has a URL, a quoted line and a retrieval date in Table 2.
3. Every non-empty phone has a named source. Any phone you cannot source:
   **delete the phone, keep the row.**
4. No phone has a sequential or repeated-digit pattern, and none begins 0804
   or 0805.
5. No phone's last 10 digits match any phone in `contacted.csv`.
6. No email matches any contacted email, case-insensitive.
7. No name or address matches any contacted practice.
8. No two rows in this batch share a phone, an email or an address.
9. Every row is a general physician practice, and Table 2 says how you know.
   **No dental, skin, eye, ENT, ortho, gynae, paeds, physio, ayurveda,
   homeopathy or veterinary rows.**
10. Every `Doctor Name Verified = yes` has a source in Table 2.
11. Every `no` row has a plausible invented name, all different, none derived
    from the clinic name, no annotations in the cell.
12. Every row passes the size and ownership tests, and Table 2 says which test
    was applied. No row rejected merely for the word "Hospital".
13. No zone exceeds its cap.
14. **Read the hooks as a set. If any two could be swapped between rows,
    rewrite both.** Every hook 25 words or fewer.
15. **Check the score spread.** If more than half the rows share one score,
    re-apply the criteria strictly and rescore.
